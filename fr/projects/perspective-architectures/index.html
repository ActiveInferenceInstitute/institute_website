Hongju Pae’s research on how artificial agents form perspectives, change through experience, and understand one another.

Hongju Pae leads this research through the Computational Emergent Alignment Research (CEAR) Lab as an Institute Research Fellow. The 2027 research prospectus, updated November 12, 2026, covers the results from the first two phases and an 18-month roadmap for Phase 3.

The research question

How can an artificial agent develop a persistent way of interpreting its environment? CEAR Lab proposes a slow latent state that accumulates the effects of experience and shapes what the agent sees as salient. The program tests how that state influences perception, action, and whether agents can understand one another over time.

Reflective Analysis on Empirical Theories in ConsciousnessResearch

Phase 1 · Subjective perspective

The first study tested a minimal architecture in which a perspective state changes more slowly than action selection. When the environment changed, the agent’s behavior could adapt quickly while the latent state retained a measurable trace of the conditions the agent had experienced. This made it possible to test the role of perspective by intervening on the latent state.

Minimal Computational Preconditions for Subjective Perspective in Artificial AgentsResearch

Phase 2 · Perception and embodiment

Project 2A · Perceptual reorganization

The perspective state fed back into perception. Agents could encode the same observation differently depending on their histories, and the system could regulate how readily it revised that state.

Project 2B · Body-grounded perspective

The model introduced an internal viability signal and connected bodily tendencies to readiness to act. The experiments distinguished how a history of bodily perturbations shaped the perspective state from how bodily tendencies influenced action.

Same World, Differently Given: History-Dependent Perceptual Reorganization in Artificial AgentsResearchBody-Grounded Perspective Formation and Conative Attunement in Artificial AgentsResearch

Phase 3 · Social attunement

Project 3A · Longitudinal human–AI interaction

This project tests whether a language-model agent can maintain a stable, revisable understanding of a human partner over time. The experiments compare the perspective architecture with retrieval-only memory and profile-based baselines.

Project 3B · Embodied multi-agent systems

This project tests whether agents with distinct histories can learn about each other and coordinate under partial observability. The program also develops measurements of causal organization and refines its account of perspective through computational phenomenology.

Perspective Latents as an Architectural Condition for Causal Emergence in Active Inference AgentsResearch

The 18-month roadmap

  • Months 1–3 establish feasibility, build prototypes for Projects 3A and 3B, and design ways to measure the effects of changing the perspective state.
  • Months 4–9 run controlled experiments in longitudinal dialogue and embodied interaction.
  • Months 10–18 extend the approaches that show reproducible effects to longer interactions and larger agent groups.
